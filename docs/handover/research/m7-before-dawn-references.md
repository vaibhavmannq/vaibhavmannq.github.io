## What each element takes from the references

### The deeper start and the walk (Amendment §4 VII; design review VII; Fingal's Cave)
- **Fingal's Cave** (Staffa) [mine, from memory; `reference-landforms.md` names it]: a sea cave about 70 m deep and
  20 m high at its mouth, its walls of **vertical basalt columns** in a lower tier (the colonnade) under a chaotic,
  finer-jointed, curved roof (the entablature). Along one side a walkway of **broken column stumps**, flat-topped,
  hexagonal, at slightly different heights, wet. The light falls off quickly inward: the deep end is near black, the
  columns near the mouth lit in grazing light that picks out every vertical edge.
- So the stop starts **in a passage behind the chamber**, about 14 m deeper, the mouth smaller and framed twice (the
  passage's edges, then the chamber's lip): "frame within a frame" (design review, my 80 % of the render).
- **The walk:** a person walking slowly in a dark cave moves at 0.3–0.6 m/s [mine]. The stop's drift covers ~14 m in
  about 30 s, eased in and out, toward the light; scrolling also moves it (so a flight never starts from the wrong
  place). Reduced motion: no walk.

### Walls and lip (owner cave 2, 3, 5; book §9; reference-landforms)
- Rock near the mouth is **lit and detailed**: the light is the dawn sky through the mouth, grazing the walls, with a
  warm bounce off the floor (owner 2, 3, 5). Faces turned to the mouth are bright, faces turned away dark; the light
  dies within ~10–15 m inward [mine: a mouth 10 m wide lights like an area light, falling off with distance squared
  once past its own width].
- Columns: vertical joints 0.5–1 m apart (Fingal's, Svartifoss; `reference-landforms.md`), cross joints every 0.3–1 m.
- Wet rock is darker and shines; dry rock is matt (book §9). Near the floor and the pool the walls are wet.
- The lip: moss on its upper edge and ledges, fern in the cracks (owner 5), **vines hanging** from the overhang as
  silhouettes against the sky. Leaves must be leaf-shaped (D5: the render's square leaves were a flaw).
- Shadows hold colour, never pure black (Amendment §3): the deepest rock is a very dark blue-grey, not 0.

### Floor and pool (owner 2, 4, 5; book §3)
- Boulders, cobbles and pebbles, rounded by the sea, gathered against the walls and at the mouth; wet sand or rock
  between (owner 2, 4, 5). Here, by Fingal's: also **column stumps** (flat hexagonal tops) near the walls.
- **A still pool** mirrors the bright mouth sharply (m2-references §4); the wet rock beside it shows only a soft
  streak toward the mouth. "The contrast between the two is what reads as wet."
- Inside a cave the air is calm: the pool is a mirror (book §2, force 0). **A drip from the roof** every few seconds
  makes one ring that spreads at 0.2–0.3 m/s and fades within a metre or two (book §3), breaking the mouth's mirror.
- The pool is darker than what it mirrors and shows the floor through it straight down (Fresnel, book §3).

### Fungi (book §6; m2-references §4; inspiration "fungi that brighten as the pointer passes", accepted D22)
- Steady, faint, blue-green; **never lamps**: they light nothing but a hint within ~0.3 m.
- In **clusters** of many small points with empty rock between, on damp walls and wood-like stumps, low down; none
  evenly spaced; sizes and brightness varied.
- The pointer: a slow wave of brightening through the nearest cluster (inspiration, D22). Real Mycena do not answer
  touch, so it stays small (≤ 1.6×), rises in ~0.3 s and dies over ~2 s: a "something noticed you", not a switch.

### The skylight (owner cave 1; reference-landforms Benagil; book §1, §4)
- Owner photo 1: a hole in the roof, a shaft of light falling onto a mound of bright moss; the shaft is visible because
  the air is misty.
- **Physics at this hour:** the sun is 10° below the horizon (book §1, nautical twilight), so **no direct beam
  exists**. Through a roof hole comes only the zenith sky: a deep blue-grey light, soft-edged, a pale column only where
  cave mist catches it. An honest shaft before dawn is cool, faint and wide, with no hard edge; a bright golden beam
  would be foreign (book §1 "the light's colour not matching the sky's").
- The floor under it shows the clearest detail in the cave: moss, wet stones (owner 1). Dust or mist motes drift in it
  very slowly (calm air, book §4).
- Built as an option with a switch (`?skylight=off`), shown running beside the stop without it.

### The view out (owner cave 2, 3; book §1, §3; owner mist 8)
- Nautical twilight: the horizon clear, a warm band low where the sun will rise (sun azimuth −12°, almost straight out
  of the mouth), the sky deep blue overhead. The sea pales with the sky: grey-blue, a broad soft road under the warm
  band (book §3, calm water narrows it).
- Outside the mouth, everything is a silhouette or pale shape: the left headland dark, the far shore a band.
- **Gives it away** (book): a straight-edged slab; a sea brighter than the sky above it; stars at full strength in a
  sky this bright (the shot has `stars: 1`; check).

## What gives a fake cave away (my list, from the above)
- A black void inside; rock with no lit edges near the mouth.
- A smooth floor; puddles that mirror like sheets of metal; puddles evenly scattered.
- Glowing things that light the rock like lamps, or sit evenly spaced.
- Square leaves; vines that hang still in a breeze or wave in a calm.
- A light shaft from a sun that is below the horizon.
- The camera turning on the spot inside a small room.
