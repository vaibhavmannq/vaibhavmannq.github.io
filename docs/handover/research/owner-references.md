# What the owner's references show (s08, 2026-11-11)

The owner added 128 files to `.superpowers/refs/` (this PC only; never in git: they are other people's photos, many
of them watermarked previews). I looked at every picture, and at 9–24 frames of every video. **This is the first
reference in this project that the builder has actually seen.** It overrides any [search] or [mine] line in the
other research files where they disagree. To view them again: `python sheets.py <refs> <out>` builds contact
sheets with ffmpeg (the script is at the end of this file).

Limits: I see video as frames, so speed and rhythm are read from frame times, not felt. No file had a note saying
what the owner liked in it; the readings below are mine, from what the pictures have in common.

## 1. The motion video (`sites-i-like/`, 23 s): pointer parallax

Three sites, each for a few seconds, the mouse moving and **no scrolling**:
- **Shopify Editions:** an island of particles with a ring of text. The whole scene turns as the pointer moves.
- **Sleep Well Creative:** flat illustrated hills, a figure, stars. The hills slide against each other with the
  pointer; small things drift across on their own.
- **Aditya's site:** as the pointer moves, the camera shifts: the piano moves about 3 % of the screen width
  against the skyline. Grass waves and butterflies fly the whole time. The sound toggle sits in the top bar
  with the menu and is switched on with one click.

So "a real interactive effect" = **the view answers the pointer at once, everywhere, before any click or scroll,
and something is always moving on its own.** This is Amendment 1 §6 step 1 (the lean). Two corrections to it:
1. It is the main effect, not a garnish. Make it clearly visible: near things should slide about 3 % of the
   screen width against far things (I had written "a degree or two"; judge by the slide, not the angle).
2. It only works with **layers in depth**: something close (grass, a rock, a branch), a subject, a far
   background. Every stop's camera needs a near layer. Golden, Sunset, Midnight and Sunrise have one; Blue hour,
   Night and First light need one added.

## 2. Aurora (13 photos)

- **Almost none is a banner parallel to the horizon.** They are folds seen from below: rays fanning out from a
  point (3, 9, 11), an S-curve or spiral overhead (2, 4, 8), a ribbon running away into the distance (6, 12).
  This confirms the corona for Night.
- **Colour is not only green:** magenta or pink tops (1, 7, 13), a red band above green (5), pale mint to white
  in the brightest cores (8, 9), teal against a blue sky. Our live aurora is one green.
- **The landscape is lit by it:** snow goes green or pink (1, 6), water carries its mirror (3, 10, 12).
- **Stars show through it,** everywhere.
- Rays are soft and of very different widths; the brightest part is usually a fold, not the lower edge.
- Three pictures have the aurora doubled in still water (3, 10, 12): the picture Night should be.
- Still photos are long exposures: brighter and more saturated than the eye sees. Right for our styled look (D2).

## 3. Waterfalls (11 photos)

- **Every one sits in an alcove:** the rock overhangs or curves round the fall, and the wall behind is in deep
  shadow, so the water is the brightest thing by contrast, not by glowing.
- **Photo 5 (Abiqua Falls, I believe) is the Night brief already built by nature:** a round bowl of basalt
  columns, moss and ferns, one fall in the middle, mist at its foot, a dark pool, trees on the rim, sky above.
  Use it as Night's first reference.
- **Split falls:** 1, 2, 6, 7, 10 are several ribbons, wide and narrow, with gaps of wet rock between.
  **Tiers:** 4 and 8 step down ledges. No fall here is one even sheet.
- **Behind the fall** (11): shot from the cave behind the water, the falls as bright strands across the opening,
  sunset beyond. This is a "spot" for Night: tap, and the camera walks behind the falls.
- **The pool:** turquoise where light reaches it (6, 7), dark and glassy in shade (2, 5), white only where the
  water lands.
- Moss and ferns on everything the spray reaches; fallen blocks, pebbles and logs at the pool's edge (2).
- Most are long exposures (silky water). Photo 9 is a fast shutter: ragged clumps, spray, a bird for scale.
  That one is the truth for motion; the silky ones are the look.

## 4. Caves (5 photos)

- **All five look out through the mouth to the sea.** Our composition is right.
- **The inside is not black.** Rock near the mouth catches warm bounce light and shows every crack (2, 3, 5);
  only the far corners go dark. Ours is black to the edge of the mouth.
- **The floor is boulders, pebbles and wet sand** (2, 4, 5), or a ribbed wet rock shelf with weed (4). Ours is a
  smooth floor with puddles.
- The mouth is rough and lopsided, with moss or fern on its lip (5).
- **Photo 3 looks out at a sea arch and stacks** (Étretat, I believe). Our cave could frame our own arch.
- **Photo 1: a shaft of light through a hole in the roof onto moss.** The owner picked this himself; the
  skylight (Amendment §4, VII) moves from "an option" to "build it".

## 5. Cliffs and columns (11 photos)

- **Photo 1 is our arch:** a basalt sea arch (Gatklettur, Iceland, I believe) with a thick leg and a thin one,
  moss on top, columns in its sides, boulders in the water beneath, low skerries beyond. Lopsided, as proposed.
- **Photo 4: basalt columns on a black beach** (Reynisfjara, I believe): columns of different heights like a
  staircase, broken tops, grey not black, and paler where dry.
- **Photo 2:** a column outcrop in a green field, grass growing right to its foot. For the cliff-top stops.
- Layered cliffs (3, 5, 6, 8, 11): flat grassy top, vertical face in bands, a **slope of fallen rock at the
  foot**, white surf where it meets the sea. Photo 5 has a headland with small stacks off its end.
- Sea caves and notches at the waterline (7, 9, 10).
- The owner's picks agree with the basalt coast (A3).

## 6. Cove and stacks (13 photos)

- **No two stacks alike, and never in an even row:** spires, blocks, lumps, some with trees on top (6, 8, 12).
- **Photo 12 is our headland line:** pointed stacks getting smaller out to sea, mist between them, a forested
  slope, a curved beach with lines of surf.
- **Coves:** a curved beach between two rocky arms (1, 7, 8, 10), turquoise water over pale sand that goes
  dark blue with depth, rocks in the shallows.
- Rock is brown, rust and grey with green on top, never one colour.
- A boulder alone on a pebble beach in the wash (10); glitter on the water behind a dark near rock (3).
- **Viewpoint:** most are taken from above, on the headland, with grass or a branch in front. Good for Dusk's
  new high camera.

## 7. Sea of cloud and sky (13 photos; several are sea, not cloud)

- **Photo 3 is the texture we want:** from above, the deck is thousands of separate cauliflower lumps with
  bright tops and blue shadow between. Not a quilt, not snow. `m2-references.md` §2 ("dark between the
  billows") is confirmed; the lumps are also **much smaller and more numerous** than ours.
- **Photo 5:** towering cloud lit orange from inside by a low sun. Clouds glow through their thin parts.
- `mist/` photo 2 is the best sea of cloud of all: a flat white deck filling a valley, the far ridges standing
  out of it as dark islands, a hard-ish edge where it meets the near hillside.
- Skies (8, 13): pink and red only on the cloud undersides, against blue-grey; stars already out above (13).
- Sea pictures (1, 4, 10, 11): deep blue open water with small ripples; turquoise and white foam at a shore.

## 8. Mist (9 photos)

- **Mist sits in layers and hides the bottoms of things.** Tree tops stand out of it; each ridge behind is
  paler than the one in front (1, 6, 9). Depth comes from the paling, with no detail needed far away.
- It clings to slopes and pours through gaps (3, 5).
- **Photo 8 is First light:** a lake at dawn, a thin flat band of mist on the water, the far shore's trees
  showing above it, pink cloud mirrored. Use it as First light's first reference.
- **Photo 7:** low sun through mist makes shafts of light between trees. For Sunrise.
- Mist is white-grey in daylight, gold in low sun, blue-grey at dusk: it takes the light's colour (confirms
  the reference book).

## 9. Meadow and flowers (12 photos)

- **The meadow photos are taken from inside the flowers:** big, sharp flowers at the bottom of the frame, the
  field running back and going soft, one tree or a tree line or a hill as the far subject (1, 2, 3, 4, 6).
  This is the new Sunset camera (A5), confirmed by the owner's own picks.
- **Mixed species in drifts:** bands and patches of one colour with others scattered through (5: white, blue,
  red in bands), never an even sprinkle. Tall grasses and seed heads stand above the flowers.
- Photo 1: a low sun over a yellow meadow with one tree: Sunset's light.
- Photo 10: a lone pink blossom tree on a green rise by water (a 3D render, not a photo): this is the owner's
  taste in a single image, and it is close to Wuthering Waves. Clean shapes, one accent colour, soft light.
- Photo 12: blue flowers under trees with light coming through.

## 10. Night sky and moon (8 photos)

- **The moon is a small sharp disc with a soft halo,** and it lights the cloud beside it (4, 5, 8).
- Photo 3: a thin crescent over black peaks in a deep teal sky. This is A2's answer in one picture (a crescent
  beside an aurora is believable).
- Photo 6: a full moon low over layered blue hills with one tree: Midnight's composition without the cloud.
- Photo 7: the Milky Way with a bright moon and a little ground mist.
- **The night sky is never black:** teal, navy or indigo, always lighter toward the horizon.

## 11. Lanterns (7 photos, 5 videos)

- **Video 4 (water lanterns at dusk) is the reference for Golden, Dusk and Night.** What it shows:
  - small paper cubes on a flat float, each about as wide as it is tall;
  - **lit from inside, brightest at the bottom** where the candle is, pale paper at the top;
  - each has its **mirror streak directly under it, broken into bars by ripples**, about as long as the
    lantern is tall;
  - handwriting on the paper (messages): our project lanterns can carry a mark or a word;
  - they drift very slowly in loose strings and turn a little; over 9 seconds they move about one lantern
    width;
  - the water is dark blue, the lanterns orange-white: the warm-on-cool contrast is the whole picture.
- Video 3 (120 fps): lanterns in clusters seen from above, each a slightly different size and tilt, some with
  a visible flame point. The flame hardly flickers.
- Video 5 and photos 3, 4: sky lanterns rising in hundreds, mirrored in water. An idea for the ending: at
  Sunrise's "light another", a lantern **rises** instead of floating.
- Hanging red lanterns (videos 1, 2; photos 1, 2, 5): they sway a few centimetres and glow deepest red at the
  edges, yellow at the core. Not for our world (they are a street), but the colour ramp is real.

## 12. Fireflies (6 photos, 3 videos)

- **The videos (real time) and the photos (long exposure) are different things.** In video: dozens of faint
  points and, at any moment, one or two bright ones; each is on for a moment and gone; they sit low, in and
  just above the plants, against near-black. Across 15–20 s the bright ones are in a different place in every
  frame. In photos the same scene is a carpet of streaks.
- So real time = sparse and dark (the reference book's "dark most of the time" is confirmed). The carpet look
  is what people remember; a short trail behind each bright firefly gets part of it honestly.
- Colour: yellow-green, the same in every clip.
- Photo 1: fireflies along a mossy stream under trees: the mood for Night's reeds.

## 13. Plankton (8 photos, 3 videos)

- **Videos 1 and 2 (surf at night):** the light is **only in the breaking crest**, an electric blue line that
  runs along the wave, flares where it breaks and is gone in a second or two; the rest of the sea is black.
- **Video 3 and photos 2–6 (shoreline):** the washed-up edge is thousands of separate blue-white sparks, dense
  along the water's last line and thinning both ways; a stream running over sand is a sparkling blue ribbon.
- Colour: electric blue to cyan-white at the brightest sparks.
- For Blue hour: sparks along every waterline (the stacks' feet, the wet flats' edge), a blue flare where a
  ripple breaks, a trail where the pointer stirs the water. Not the even sprinkle across open water that the
  still has. (The reference book is confirmed.)

## 14. What the collection says about the owner's taste (my reading)

1. **Layers:** nearly every picture has something close, a subject, and a far distance.
2. **A second colour:** green with magenta, blue with orange, pink with teal. Never one hue.
3. **Water that mirrors.**
4. **Shadows with something in them:** almost no pure black anywhere in 128 files.
5. **Real places with a clean, graphic shape** (the lone tree, the arch, the stack).

The owner chose *real behaviour, styled colour* (A2, D27). So our falls and water are lit by the sky and aurora,
but that light may be richer and bluer than life; nothing glows by itself except living things and flames.

## The viewing script

`sheets.py <refs_dir> <out_dir>`: for each folder, thumbnails every image with ffmpeg (it reads AVIF) and tiles
six to a sheet with file names; for each video, 9 frames (24 if longer than 30 s) with their times. Needs ffmpeg
on PATH and Pillow. Keep the output outside the repo. A denser cut of one video:
`ffmpeg -ss <start> -t <length> -i <video> -vf "fps=1.25,scale=960:-1,tile=3x3" -frames:v 1 out.jpg`.
