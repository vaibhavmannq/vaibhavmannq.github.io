**Task 3, First light's mist and the surf foam.**
- `src/world/mist.ts`: the mist is folded into the scene's one fog (no draw, no material). The density falls as
  exp(−k·y) over the sea (k = ln 10 / top) and is integrated along the ray from the eye; patches tens of metres
  across (two sine products) drift with the wind, capped at 0.08 m/s. Colour: the fog's own (the sky's horizon, D17).
- `hours.ts MIST`: First light 1, Sunrise 0.35, every other hour 0; blended with the light, so it gathers on the way
  in from Before dawn and thins toward Sunrise. Full: 0.01 /m at the sea, top 2.0 m; at Sunrise 0.0035 /m, top 1.2 m.
- `sea.ts foamLevel(beaufort)`: edge line 0.35 at force 0, 0.68 at force 1, full from force 2; the swash sheet 0 at
  force 0, 0.33 at force 1, full from force 2.
- Tests: `tests/unit/mist.test.ts` (mist only at First light and Sunrise, never between two mistless hours; top
  ≤ 2.5 m everywhere, 1–2 m at First light; thickens in, thins out; reads as a band from First light's eye; drift
  ≤ 0.1 m/s with the wind; foam by force).

Rulings:
- **R53:** the built-in `exponentialHeightFogFactor` was tried first (plan) and dropped.
  - Why: it fogs each surface by its own depth under the top times its view depth, and ignores where the eye is.
    From First light's eye (0.55 m, inside the layer) it gave a thin bright line at the waterline, not a band
    (`.superpowers/m3/t3a.jpg`). The closed-form layer integral gives the band (`t3b.jpg`, `t3c-crop.png`).
  - Cost if wrong: a few more ALU ops in every material that takes fog; not measurable (below).
- **R54:** the foam follows the wind force everywhere, not only at First light. Golden, Dusk and Sunrise are force 1,
  so their shore foam is about half and the swash sheet mostly gone (reference book §2–3: force 0–1 water is a
  mirror with no crests). Golden's shore before/after: `t3a.jpg` row 3; the change is small there.
  - Cost if wrong: the owner liked the old foam at Golden; one constant brings it back.
- **R55:** First light's top is 2.0 m (plan: 1–2 m deep, ≤ 2.5). 1.6 m read as a thin stripe at the headland's
  foot, 150 m away; 2.0 m gives the foot a body.
