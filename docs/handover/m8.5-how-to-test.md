# M8.5: how to test the world yourself

Written in s23 (2026-11-21) for M8.5's rounds. The build is the `world` branch, served from the laptop. Nothing is online:
the live site is still the old one until M10.

## 1. Start the build

- If a session is running, I serve it for you. Otherwise double-click
  `.superpowers/m85/start-test-build.cmd` (in the repo folder). A black window opens; keep it open while you test.
  Closing it stops the build. If it says the port is in use, the build is already being served: just open the link.

## 2. Laptop (Chrome)

- Open **http://localhost:4181** in Chrome.
- Scroll with the mouse wheel or the arrow keys. **Next** (bottom right) rides to the next place. Down/Right/Page Down
  ride forward, Up/Left/Page Up ride back.
- Drag with the mouse to look around. Small glints in the scene are **spots**: click one to walk there.
- At **V Night** the floating lanterns are the projects: hover lifts one, click opens its card.
- **Sound off / Sound on** is top right. Sound is off until you press it.
- The numerals on the right (I–IX) jump to a place.

## 3. Phone (Brave), over USB

The phone must be plugged in by USB, because only `localhost` gives the phone the fast graphics path (WebGPU).
Over Wi-Fi it would fall back to the slower one (WebGL2), which is not what visitors get.

1. Plug the phone in (USB debugging on, as before).
2. Double-click `start-test-build.cmd` again (or tell me "phone is connected"). It links the phone to the laptop.
3. In Brave on the phone, open **http://localhost:4181**.
4. Swipe up/down to travel, drag sideways to look around, tap glints and lanterns. Try it held upright and sideways.

If the phone is unplugged and plugged back in, run step 2 again.

## 4. `?hud`, the measuring panel (optional)

Add `?hud` to the address: **http://localhost:4181/?hud** (phone: **http://localhost:4181/?hud** too).
A small dark panel appears top right, under the Sound button:

| Line | What it means |
|---|---|
| `60 fps  worst 18.2 ms` | frames per second, and the slowest frame in the last second. 60 and under ~25 ms is smooth. |
| `draws 43  tris 1.81 M` | how much the GPU draws (for me; ignore). |
| `low  webgpu` | the quality tier the site chose, and the graphics path (`webgpu` or `webgl2`). |
| `stop III` / `flight III→IV 0.42` | where you are: at a place, or flying between two (0 = start, 1 = end). |
| `world 3.44 MB` | how much of the world has been downloaded (reads 0 on a repeat visit; that is the cache). |

- The buttons **low / mid / high** switch quality on the spot, to compare. The site normally picks one by itself.
- **stills off/on** lays the fallback pictures over the world (what a visitor without a GPU sees).
- **30 fps after 8 seconds of not touching anything is on purpose:** the world slows down when no one is moving, to save
  power. Move the mouse or touch the screen and it goes back to 60.
- If something felt slow, a note like "Dusk→Blue hour, around 0.4, worst 120 ms, low" helps me find it fast.

## 5. Handy links

Add `#` and a place name to start there: `#golden`, `#sunset`, `#dusk`, `#bluehour`, `#night`, `#midnight`,
`#beforedawn`, `#firstlight`, `#sunrise`. Example: **http://localhost:4181/?hud#night**.
To feel a first visit as a stranger does, use a private (incognito) window: nothing is cached there.
