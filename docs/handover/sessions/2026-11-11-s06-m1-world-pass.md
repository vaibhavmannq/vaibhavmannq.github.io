## Learned

- The strict "nothing any camera sees may change" mask protected 45 % of the map, including all land under the flights
  (Midnight looks west at ranges 3–7 km away). Rules that made M1 possible: protect exactly within 1.5 km; Midnight's
  sea of cloud hides what is under it. → 17 % protected, two-thirds of the flight land free.
- coast.py's ground costs ~31 µs per point: any check that casts rays needs the ceiling shortcut and the
  "only re-cast rays that touched changed ground" shortcut (both proven equal to brute force in test_views.py).
  Full world checks take ~12 minutes on this 2-core laptop: run them in the background.
- three 0.186 node materials: InstancedMesh.setColorAt colours did not reach the shader, and a uniformArray / small
  DataTexture updated every frame rendered its start-up values. What works: data in vertex attributes, and data painted
  into a texture before its first upload.
- A photo's exact horizon row has the sun and cloud edges in it: use a blurred band (typical colour, not a mean of raw
  light, which glare rules) for anything "horizon coloured".
- Prior art (owner asked for web searches): fading distance into the real sky colour instead of a flat fog colour is
  what browser open-world builders settle on; distant trees are usually impostors with a dithered fade (idea for M8).
